What to look for

A GMP bioreactor and cleanroom suite. Operators run a batch from HMIs on a process DCS; an MES pulls recipes and writes electronic batch records; a serialization/packaging line runs downstream; a bioreactor SIS protects against over-pressure and runaway reactions.

  1. HMI signed in with a shared 'operator' account; no per-user identity, so batch actions can't be attributed.

    3-3SR 1.1Human user identification and authentication4-2CR 1.1Human user identification and authentication84.09OperationSIS access control and independence from the BPCS

  2. Batch/recipe parameters can be changed directly at the HMI with no authorization step or second check.

    3-3SR 2.1Authorization enforcement3-3SR 3.4Software and information integrityPLC#8Validate HMI input variables at the PLC level, not only at the HMI

  3. Unmanaged engineering laptop and USB drive plugged straight into the control cabinet / engineering port.

    3-3SR 2.3Use control for portable and mobile devices4-2CR 2.13Use of physical diagnostic and test interfaces3-3SR 3.2Malicious code protection

  4. Bioreactor over-pressure trip left in BYPASS with no management-of-change record.

    84.09OperationBypass / override management for the SISPLC#2Track operating modesPLC#15Define a safe process state in case of a PLC restart

  5. MES/IT and bioreactor PLCs share one flat VLAN — no zone boundary between business and process.

    3-3SR 5.1Network segmentation3-3SR 5.2Zone boundary protection4-2CR 5.1Network segmentation

  6. PLC/HMI still on the default vendor credential printed on a sticky note.

    3-3SR 1.5Authenticator management3-3SR 1.7Strength of password-based authentication4-2CR 1.7Strength of password-based authentication

  7. Contractor exports historian/batch data to a personal USB drive with no control on the data flow.

    3-3SR 4.1Information confidentiality3-3SR 5.3General purpose person-to-person communication restrictions3-3SR 2.3Use control for portable and mobile devices

  8. GOOD PRACTICE: serialization line PLC runs a checksum / integrity check on its program and alarms on change.

    PLC#5Use cryptographic and/or checksum integrity checks for PLC code3-3SR 3.4Software and information integrity
